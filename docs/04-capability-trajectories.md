# 04 — Capability Trajectories

## 1. Unit of analysis

The proposed unit of analysis is the **capability trajectory** rather than the country, project, laboratory, or institution taken as a whole.

A capability trajectory follows a specific scientific function over time.

Example:

```text
instrument procurement
→ installation
→ initial training
→ routine operation
→ calibration
→ failure
→ diagnosis
→ repair
→ software / protocol update
→ staff turnover
→ local retraining
→ new research use
```

At every step, the same questions can be asked:

- Who has the knowledge?
- Who has permission to act?
- Who can validate the result?
- Who owns or controls the necessary data?
- Which standards or external services are indispensable?
- Which records make the transition reconstructible?
- Can the capability be transmitted to another person or institution?
- What happens when a critical dependency changes?

## 2. Capability profile

Use a multidimensional profile rather than a composite “capacity score.”

| Dimension | Empirical question |
|---|---|
| Operate | Can local actors use the infrastructure reliably? |
| Maintain | Can they diagnose, repair, replace, and sustain it? |
| Validate | Can they independently interpret and assess outputs? |
| Adapt | Can protocols, methods, code, or components be modified? |
| Govern | Can priorities, access rules, standards, or agendas be influenced? |
| Transmit | Can competence be reproduced across personnel turnover? |
| Reconfigure | Can the activity continue when suppliers, funding, people, or partnerships change? |

## 3. Dependency profile

Capability should be paired with an explicit dependency map.

| Dependency | Questions |
|---|---|
| Finance | Who funds recurrent operation, replacement, and expansion? |
| Hardware | Are critical components repairable or substitutable? |
| Software | Who controls source, licenses, updates, models, or cloud services? |
| Data | Who collects, stores, grants access to, and can reuse data? |
| Standards | Which external standards determine interoperability and legitimacy? |
| Expertise | Where does scarce tacit knowledge reside? |
| Authority | Which decisions require external approval? |

## 4. Reconfiguration events as natural tests

Capabilities become especially visible when systems are perturbed.

Useful events include:

- staff turnover;
- equipment failure;
- contract expiration;
- change of supplier;
- withdrawal or reduction of funding;
- new scientific question;
- new data-governance regulation;
- sanctions or export controls;
- software deprecation;
- institutional restructuring.

A capability that only exists in stable conditions may be operationally useful but institutionally fragile.

## 5. No universal ladder

The framework must not imply:

```text
operate < maintain < modify < reproduce
```

as a universal hierarchy of development.

A research institution may rationally specialize in interpretation while relying on external satellite infrastructure. Another may choose to localize hardware maintenance. Another may pool expensive capabilities regionally.

The relevant questions are:

```text
Who can do what?
Who decides this division of functions?
Can the arrangement be renegotiated?
What becomes possible over time?
```
